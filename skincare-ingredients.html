Understanding Skincare Ingredients

A beginner's guide to choosing products with confidence

THE SKINCARE EDIT

Walking through the skincare aisle or browsing beauty products online can feel overwhelming. There are countless ingredients, product claims and routines to consider. Understanding a few common skincare ingredients can make shopping easier and help you choose products more thoughtfully.

You do not need an elaborate routine or a cupboard full of products. The most useful starting point is understanding what a product is designed to do, whether it suits your skin and how to use it correctly.

1. Hyaluronic Acid: An Ingredient for Hydration

Hyaluronic acid is a humectant, meaning it helps attract and retain water in the skin. It is commonly included in serums, moisturisers and other hydrating products.

You may want to explore products containing hyaluronic acid if your skin often feels dry or tight. It is available in different product formulations, so check the instructions and choose one that fits your routine.

What to check when shopping:

  • Whether the product is intended for facial use.
  • Its texture and compatibility with your moisturiser.
  • The directions provided by the manufacturer.
  • Whether the product fits your budget and needs.

2. Niacinamide: A Versatile Skincare Ingredient

Niacinamide is a form of vitamin B3. It is commonly used in skincare formulations designed to support the skin barrier and improve the appearance of uneven skin tone.

It can be found in serums, moisturisers and other skincare products. The concentration and overall formulation can differ between products, so a higher percentage is not automatically a better choice.

Before purchasing:

  • Check the concentration if it is provided.
  • Read the complete ingredient list.
  • Consider the other active ingredients in the formula.
  • Introduce a new product gradually.

3. Vitamin C: An Antioxidant Used in Skincare

Vitamin C is an antioxidant used in skincare products, particularly those formulated to improve the appearance of uneven skin tone and dullness.

Different forms of vitamin C are used in skincare. Some formulations may be more suitable for particular preferences or skin needs than others.

When shopping, pay attention to the product's packaging, storage instructions and expiry information. Some vitamin C formulations can change colour as they age or become exposed to air and light. Follow the manufacturer's guidance.

4. Salicylic Acid: An Ingredient Often Used for Oily Skin

Salicylic acid is a beta hydroxy acid (BHA). It is commonly included in cleansers and other products designed for oily or blemish-prone skin.

Because it is an exfoliating ingredient, using too many exfoliating products together can cause dryness or irritation. Start with the manufacturer's recommended frequency rather than assuming that daily use is necessary.

A helpful reminder: If you are new to exfoliating ingredients, introduce one product at a time. Avoid combining several strong active products without understanding how they may interact.

5. Ceramides: Supporting the Skin Barrier

Ceramides are lipids naturally found in the skin barrier. They help the skin retain moisture and maintain its protective function. Skincare products containing ceramides are commonly used in moisturisers and barrier-supporting formulations.

They may be worth considering if you prefer moisturising products designed to support dry or easily irritated skin. Look at the full formula rather than judging a product by one ingredient alone.

How to Read a Skincare Ingredient List

A product's ingredient list can tell you a great deal about what you are buying. Here are some useful things to look for:

  • Product purpose: Is it a cleanser, moisturiser, sunscreen or treatment?
  • Ingredient names: Look for the ingredients you are interested in and understand their intended role.
  • Fragrance: Check whether fragrance is included if you prefer fragrance-free products.
  • Concentration: Some products state the percentage of particular active ingredients. Not every ingredient needs a high concentration.
  • Directions: Read how often the manufacturer recommends using the product.
  • Warnings: Check any precautions, storage advice and compatibility information.

Choosing Products for Your Skin

Your skin's needs, preferences and tolerance matter when choosing skincare. General skin-type descriptions can help you narrow down your options, but they are not a substitute for individual advice.

If your skin tends to feel dry

You may prefer moisturisers with ingredients such as glycerin, hyaluronic acid and ceramides. Look for a texture that feels comfortable and provides enough moisture for your needs.

If your skin tends to feel oily

You may prefer lightweight moisturisers and cleansers that remove excess oil without leaving your skin feeling uncomfortable. Avoid assuming that oily skin does not need moisturiser.

If your skin is sensitive

Consider products with straightforward formulations and check ingredient lists carefully. Fragrance-free products may be worth exploring if fragrance tends to bother your skin. Patch-test new products according to their instructions.

If your skin is combination

You may find that different areas of your face have different needs. Choose products that feel comfortable across your skin rather than relying only on product labels.

Common Skincare Shopping Mistakes

  • Buying too many products at once: It can make it difficult to understand which products suit you.
  • Following every trend: Popular products are not automatically suitable for everyone.
  • Ignoring instructions: Different ingredients have different usage recommendations.
  • Choosing by packaging alone: Attractive packaging does not guarantee that a product suits your needs.
  • Expecting immediate results: Many skincare changes take time, and results vary between individuals.
  • Overlooking sunscreen: Broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher is an important part of daytime skin protection.

Your Skincare Shopping Checklist

Before purchasing a new skincare product, ask yourself:

  • What do I want this product to do?
  • Is it appropriate for my skin and current routine?
  • Have I checked the ingredients and directions?
  • Do I understand how frequently to use it?
  • Is the seller reputable?
  • Have I checked the product size, price and expiry information?
  • Can I afford to use and replace it consistently?

Final Thoughts

Understanding skincare ingredients does not require becoming an expert in every product or trend. Learning what a few common ingredients do, reading labels and choosing products carefully can make your shopping decisions more informed.

Start with the basics, introduce new products gradually and pay attention to how your skin responds. If you have a persistent skin concern or experience a reaction, seek advice from a qualified healthcare professional or dermatologist.
